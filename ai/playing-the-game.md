# Playing without the UI

Human start recipes: [HOW-TO-PLAY.md](../HOW-TO-PLAY.md).

The React app, curl, and MCP all drive **one class**: `GameService`.

```
                         GameService
           newGame · join · state · options · act · save · load
                               |
              +----------------+----------------+
              |                                 |
         LocalGameClient                  HTTP :8472
         (default npm run dev)                  |
                                          +-----+------+
                                          |            |
                                   ?server= UI    MCP --url
                                   curl           (or private --mcp)
```

`options()` is the list of legal actions. Miner upgrades are `{ "type": "upgradeMiner", "upgrade": "speed" | "damage" | "fireRate" | "carry" | "count" | "production" }`. `count` starts at 1 for the miner already at the base, and each purchase adds one. A destroyed miner is built again until that number is full. `production` is units built each second on the miner bar. A queued miner costs `ships.units`. Rate 1 fills that bar in 60 seconds. A miner is drawn as a prebuilt ship: the designer core, a thruster, and a miner laser. That shot is a short yellow beam. A friendly miner reaches `ships.minerRange`. Rival miners and fighters still use `ships.weaponRange`. Miner upgrades still set its speed, damage, fire rate, carry, and the rest. Each leveled upgrade is a table of rows. The saved level picks the row. `cost` on that row is the next purchase, the last row is the maximum, and `crystal` marks a crystal purchase. A one-time unlock is still a single cost. Mining upgrades are `{ "type": "upgradeMining", "upgrade": "unlock" | "size" | "spawnRate" | "goldenSpawnRate" | "golden" | "tractorBeam" }`. The Mining sidebar button is always shown. That page offers only `unlock` until it is bought, and `unlock` is the only mining action until then. `{ "type": "upgradeMining", "upgrade": "automine" }` is offered only after the tractor beam is owned and costs `mining.automine.cost`. It clears every asteroid, stops further spawns, removes every player miner and the miner queue, and pays `mining.automine.payout` all at once every `mining.automine.everyMs`. Rivals do not fly back to their base. The Miner card hides, and miner purchases and the earlier mining purchases are no longer offered. The Mining button stays. `{ "type": "upgradeMining", "upgrade": "moneyConverter" }` is offered only then and costs `mining.moneyConverter.cost`. Once it is owned, money above `mining.moneyConverter.above` becomes one crystal and the whole balance is spent. The Mining button then hides. `skipLevelOne` does not buy automine or the converter. `{ "type": "unlockDesigner" }` costs `designer.cost` and adds the Ship Designer sidebar. `{ "type": "placeComponent", "component", "x", "y", "rotation" }` bolts that part onto the grid. The click is the part's local (0, 0), rotation is 0, 90, 180, or 270, and a mount must touch the opposite mount of a square already connected to the core. `{ "type": "saveDesign" }` copies that grid into the game and names it ship1, ship2, and so on, and is refused when the ship's energy is negative. That latest copy is what the rest of the game uses. `{ "type": "loadDesign", "name": "ship1" }` puts that saved ship back on the grid and leaves the fleet's ship as the latest save. Editing the grid does not change it until `saveDesign` runs again. Export writes the grid to `ship-design.json`. Import replaces the grid from that file and leaves the copied production design until `saveDesign`. `{ "type": "resetDesign" }` clears the grid the same way. Delete is a tool: once it is selected, clicking a part takes that part off. `{ "type": "chooseProduction", "design": "fighter" | "ship1" }` picks which ship a bay builds. `fighter` is the default. `ship1`, `ship2`, and so on are the saved designs, and each bay can pick a different one. `{ "type": "unlockBay" }` spends the next crystal cost in `designer.bays` to open another bay. `{ "type": "chooseProduction", "design": "fighter" | "ship1", "bay": 1 }` picks that bay's ship. `bay` is the list index, and omitting it means the first bay. Every open bay builds at once, at 60% of the fighter production rate. `{ "type": "upgradeBay", "bay": 0 }` buys one production level for that bay. Two levels, paid in money, $2000 then $4000. Each level multiplies that bay's rate by 1.5. The fighter count still caps how many ships are out or in progress. A saved ship is offered once that name exists. The Fleet Design screen shows that ship's build cost and shape. A saved design flies at its speed times `ships.speed`, and on the map it is those colored squares. Each shot deals `designer.weaponDamage`. Each gun type keeps its own timer. Extra guns of that type shorten its wait. After the designer is owned, the Weapons tab on Ship Upgrades sells a laser and a missile. The laser is long range and spends a whole energy block. Its shot hits immediately and draws a line from the ship to the target, blue for the player and red for a rival. The line stays for 200 ms. Its upgrades raise that range and lower the energy it draws. Those upgrades cost crystals, starting at 5 and doubling. The missile stays hidden until a ship destroys an enemy corvette that carries one. A base cannon kill does not count. `{ "type": "unlockWeapon", "weapon": "missile" }` then costs one crystal. Its fire-rate and damage upgrades cost crystals, starting at 5 and doubling. The missile is medium range. It flies at half the shot speed and is drawn as a longer projectile. One missile starts at 30 damage and fires every 5000 ms. The cannon comes with the designer. It starts at 10 damage and fires every 1000 ms, and the same tab raises its fire rate and damage. The first buy of each cannon upgrade costs money. Later buys cost crystals, starting at 5 and doubling. Ship Upgrades also sells a medium engine and a large engine. They cover more cells, draw more energy, and push harder than the stock thruster. A missile hit also deals half that damage to enemy ships in a round patch of map cells, about three and a half cells from the impact. Those cells light up, then fade. A ship closes until its shortest powered gun is in range, then stops. Longer guns fire on the way in, and only guns that reach add to that type's fire rate. The default fighter still uses the fighter speed, damage, attack-rate, and health upgrades. It is drawn with the same hull as a miner. A saved design's health is the core plus its structural and armor blocks, and the fighter health upgrade does not change those ships. Each placed part adds build units, the same production units a fighter takes, and the designer shows that total. Each part also adds weight. Thrusters add thrust. Speed is thrust divided by weight. Every part consumes energy, and energy parts grant more than one part uses. `{ "type": "removeComponent", "id" }` takes a part off when the rest of the ship stays connected to the core. `skipLevelOne` does not buy the designer. `{ "type": "upgradesToShipDesign" }` maxes miner speed, damage, fire rate, carry, count, and production; mining unlock, size, spawn rate, golden asteroids, golden spawn rate, and the tractor beam; fighter speed, attack rate, health, damage, count, and production; and enemy spawn rate, capacity, health, damage, and attack rate. It also unlocks the ship designer, the laser, and the medium and large engines, and it turns on automine. It does not unlock the missile or buy missile upgrades. Each level uses the same effects as buying it, and nothing is charged. An upgrade added later is not on that list. `goldenSpawnRate` is offered only after golden asteroids are owned. `{ "type": "upgradeFighter", "upgrade": "speed" | "attackRate" | "health" | "damage" | "count" | "production" }` buys one of its stats. `count` starts at 0 and is how many fighters are kept. The first purchase is free, the next costs `count.cost`, and the price doubles until `count.max` fighters. After that, `count.crystalLevels` purchases cost crystals, starting at `count.crystal` and doubling, and each adds `count.crystalStep` fighters. A destroyed fighter is built again until that number is full. `production` is units built each second on the fighter bar. A queued fighter costs `fighter.units`. The starting rate fills that bar in 25 seconds. Each production purchase makes the next build take 70% as long. These are offered only after the tractor beam is owned. Speed, attack rate, health, and damage are on Ship Upgrades. Count and production stay on the Default fighter page. That sidebar button hides once both are maxed. The Ship Upgrades button hides once speed, attack rate, health, and damage are maxed. Each fleet bay has its own capacity. Bay 1 uses that count. An extra bay starts with `designer.bayCapacity.start` ships, and further purchases add `step` up to `max`, paid in money that starts at `cost` and doubles. `{ "type": "upgradeEnemy", "upgrade": "spawnRate" | "capacity" | "health" | "damage" | "attackRate" }` is offered only after the tractor beam is owned, when the Enemy tab appears. That screen opens onto a menu of Fighter and Corvette. Spawn rate and Capacity are on the Fighter page. `spawnRate` shortens how long a destroyed rival waits to return. `capacity` shows the live rival fleet cap. That cap starts at 1. Reaching three player miners doubles it, and golden asteroids multiply it by three. The first Capacity purchase sets the cap to 10, and each later purchase adds 5. While the fleet is under that cap, the spawn-rate clock produces one ship at a time. At the cap, that clock is off. Health, damage, and attack rate raise the rival fighters. Each Capacity, health, damage, and attack-rate purchase multiplies the payout for a destroyed rival fighter by 1.3, and those factors compound. Spawn rate does not. The Enemy menu shows the net multiplier. A ship that destroys a rival fighter pays the fighter bounty times that multiplier, rounded to the nearest dollar. A base cannon kill pays nothing. `{ "type": "unlockCorvette" }` is on the Corvette page until it is bought. After that, `{ "type": "upgradeCorvette", "upgrade": "spawnRate" | "capacity" | "health" | "damage" | "attackRate" | "speed" | "missiles" }` raises that ship. Each of those costs crystals, starting at 5 and doubling. The unlock starts a second clock that builds one corvette at a time, up to its own cap, and halves how fast enemy fighters are produced. The hull is three cells wide, with three engines abreast at the stern. Its cannon reaches half again as far as a fighter gun, and it keeps closing until it is 15% of that range nearer. Shots alternate port and starboard. Attack rate 1 fires every 375 ms, and each shot picks the enemy closest to that muzzle, so both guns may choose the same ship. `missiles` is one purchase. Corvettes already out, and every corvette built after it, carry a missile and fire it twice as often as a player's rate-1 missile. A direct hit deals `corvette.missileDamage`, and other ships in the blast take half of that. That purchase also doubles the crystals a corvette pays. A ship that destroys a corvette is paid the corvette bounty and that crystal count. A base cannon kill pays neither. Crystals show in the header next to money. `{ "type": "skipLevelOne" }` maxes every miner upgrade and every upgrade on the Mining page, including its unlock, without spending money. Levels already bought stay put, and a second use changes nothing. `{ "type": "claimGift" }` pays the sidebar gift. The first payout is `bank.gift`, the next claim pays `bank.giftNext`, and each claim after that doubles. `{ "type": "claimCrystalGift" }` pays the sidebar crystal button. The first payout is `bank.crystalGift`, and each claim doubles the next one.
`act` returns `{ ok: false, reason, error }` for anything else.

## Shared play

```bash
cd incremental-game
npm run server:http
npm run dev
# browser: http://localhost:5173/?server=http://127.0.0.1:8472
```

Opening `?server=` joins if a game exists (`POST /join`), and calls
`POST /new-game` when the server has none. The response includes a token.
The browser stores it in `sessionStorage` and sends `X-Incremental-Token`
on later requests. Refresh resumes that seat.

Save is the game only. Tokens are not in the file. New Game and Load clear
seats, then sit the caller again.

The UI polls `/state` every 2 seconds and reloads `/save` when `revision`
changes.

## curl

```bash
PORT=8472
TOKEN=$(curl -s -X POST http://localhost:$PORT/new-game \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' -d '{}' \
  | python3 -c 'import json,sys; print(json.load(sys.stdin)["data"]["token"])')

curl -s http://localhost:$PORT/state -H "X-Incremental-Token: $TOKEN"
curl -s http://localhost:$PORT/options -H "X-Incremental-Token: $TOKEN"
curl -s http://localhost:$PORT/save -H "X-Incremental-Token: $TOKEN"
```

Once rivals are attacking, `{ "type": "upgradeDefense", "upgrade": "capacity" | "health" | "attackRate" | "damage" }` buys a defense platform. Capacity is a single purchase. Health, attack rate, and damage are refused until that purchase, and those rows spend crystals. Buying capacity places the platform immediately. The next one keeps building on a one-minute clock even while one is out, and it is not deployed until the slot is free. One finished platform waits, and the build bar stays full until that slot opens. Fighters and miners build the same way. It drifts slowly from the player base to just short of a third of the map, then stays there. Other ships fly through it. It shoots a large cannon bolt at 1.125 times the base cannon range. Attack rate 1 fires every 2 seconds. Damage starts at 120 and reaches 300.

`GET /` prints the route list.
