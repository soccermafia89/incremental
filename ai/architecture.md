# Architecture

Dependencies point inward. No React in `src/core/`.

| Layer | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| `src/core/game/` | Glue. `Game` delegates to the map. `Clock` chops time into steps. |
| `src/core/map/` | The live map: layout, spawn clock, and the step order. |
| `src/core/designer/` | Ship designer. `geometry.ts` turns parts and checks mounts. `Design.ts` is the grid. `Library.ts` is the named ships. The designer opens on the library: new, edit, delete, and import. Edit opens the grid and save replaces that ship. New design opens a blank grid and save appends the next name. Delete removes a ship and renames the rest ship1, ship2, and so on. A bay that was building a deleted ship returns to the default fighter. A later ship keeps its place under the new name. Save is refused when the ship's energy is negative. Fleet Design lists every saved ship. Each bay picks the default fighter or one of those names. A second bay costs 20 crystals and builds another ship at the same time. Later bays unlock from the same list. Each bay builds at 60% of the fighter production rate. Two money purchases on that bay, $2000 then $4000, each multiply that bay's rate by 1.5. Each bay keeps its own ships, so an extra bay no longer borrows from the first bay's cap. A ship launched from the saved design is drawn on the map as those colored squares. Its health is the core plus its structural and armor blocks. The grid screen is `src/ui/designer/`. Catalog and unlock cost are `src/data/designer/`. |
| `src/core/miner/` | Miners: flight, jobs, upgrades, the once-a-second duty check. Loot in a map cell is taken by a random ship in that cell that still has room. After the tractor beam, rival miners hunt player ships, and the fighter hunts rivals. A fighter with no rival left flies to the enemy base and shoots it. A hostile rival with no player ship left flies to the player base and shoots it. Neither base has health, so those shots do not harm it. A cannon shot whose target is destroyed keeps flying on its last heading until it leaves the map. A missile whose target is destroyed locks onto the closest ship of the other side. The cap starts at 1. Reaching three player miners doubles it, golden asteroids multiply it by three, and the enemy capacity upgrade sets it to 10 on the first purchase and adds 5 after that. While the fleet is under that cap, the spawn-rate clock is on and produces one ship at a time. At the cap, that clock is off. Buying the corvette on the enemy page starts a second clock that builds one corvette at a time, up to that ship's own cap, and halves how fast enemy fighters are produced. A corvette is a narrow ship: the body is three cells wide, and three engines sit abreast at the stern. Its cannon reaches half again as far as a fighter gun, and it keeps closing until it is 15% of that range nearer. Shots alternate port and starboard. Attack rate 1 fires every `corvette.shotMs` (375 ms), and each shot picks the enemy closest to that muzzle, so both guns may choose the same ship. Buying its missile upgrade fits a missile on every corvette already out and on every one built after that. That missile launches twice as often as a player's rate-1 missile. A direct hit deals `corvette.missileDamage`, and other ships in the blast take half of that. The purchase also doubles the crystals a ship kill pays. A ship that destroys one is paid its bounty and that crystal count. A base cannon kill pays neither. Corvette damage is its own upgrade, and golden asteroids and the tractor beam do not multiply it. Each capacity purchase multiplies the bounty for a destroyed rival fighter by 1.3, as do the health, damage, and attack-rate upgrades, which also raise the rival ships. That damage number is the base. Golden asteroids and the tractor beam each multiply it by 2, and the shot uses that product. Spawn rate does not. Rate 1 is one a minute, and each purchase doubles the rate so the wait halves, up to 128. |
| `src/core/base/` | The player bank. Each base cannon rests in stasis until an enemy enters `cannon.wake`. Combat then shoots every enemy inside `cannon.range`, `cannon.rate` times a second, for `cannon.damage`, and returns to stasis when that circle is empty. The shot is a laser at `cannon.shotSpeed`, so it crosses that range in one frame. |
| `src/core/asteroid/` | Asteroid field, and what happens when a shot breaks one. |
| `src/core/ore/` | Ore nuggets. |
| `src/core/defense/` | Defense platforms. Shown once rivals attack. One capacity purchase, paid in money, then health, attack rate, and damage. Those three rows spend crystals. Buying capacity places the first platform immediately. The next one keeps building every `defense.buildMs` even while one is deployed. One finished platform waits, and the build bar stays full until the slot is free. A bar at the bottom of the map stays once that line is unlocked and fills while it builds. Automine removes the miner bar. Fighters and miners do the same: production continues at capacity, one finished ship waits, and a destroyed ship releases it. It appears beside the player base, drifts right at `defense.speed` to just short of a third of the map, and stays there. Other ships fly through it. It shoots a large cannon bolt at `cannon.range` times `defense.rangeScale`. Attack rate 1 waits `defense.shotMs` (2s). Damage starts at 120 and reaches 300. |
| `src/core/shot/` | Shots in flight. |
| `src/core/motion/` | Shared movement. |
| `src/core/persistence/` | Save and load. |
| `src/data/` | YAML settings, one file per module: map, asteroids, golden, ships, fighter, enemy, rival, ore, cannon, bank, upgrades, mining, designer, defense. Fighter stats are `src/data/fighter/upgrades.yaml`. Enemy spawn rate and capacity are `src/data/enemy/upgrades.yaml`. Defense platforms are `src/data/defense/`. `GameConfig` loads them. |
| `src/service/` | `GameService`: the only mutator the UI, HTTP, and MCP use |
| `src/server/` | Thin HTTP and MCP adapters over `GameService`. MCP protocol, stdio, and the HTTP proxy are separate modules. |
| `src/ui/` | React shell. `shell/` is the frame and the client session. Inline `style={{}}`. |

`GameService` is the mutable surface: `newGame`, `join`, `state`, `options`,
`act`, `save`, `load`. HTTP routes and MCP tools are generated from
`src/service/api.ts`. Adding an operation means adding it there once.

`options()` returns the legal actions. Miner upgrades are `{ "type": "upgradeMiner", "upgrade": "speed" | "damage" | "fireRate" | "carry" | "count" | "production" }`. `count` starts at 1 for the miner already at the base, and each purchase adds one. A destroyed miner is built again until that number is full. `production` is units built each second on the miner bar. A queued miner costs `ships.units`. Rate 1 fills that bar in 60 seconds. A miner is drawn as a prebuilt ship: the designer core, a thruster, and a miner laser. That shot is a short yellow beam. A friendly miner reaches `ships.minerRange`. Rival miners and fighters still use `ships.weaponRange`. Miner upgrades still set its speed, damage, fire rate, carry, and the rest. Each leveled upgrade is a table of rows. The saved level picks the row. `cost` on that row is the next purchase, the last row is the maximum, and `crystal` marks a crystal purchase. A one-time unlock is still a single cost. Mining upgrades are `{ "type": "upgradeMining", "upgrade": "unlock" | "size" | "spawnRate" | "goldenSpawnRate" | "golden" | "tractorBeam" }`. The Mining sidebar button is always shown. That page offers only `unlock` until it is bought, and `unlock` is the only mining action until then. `{ "type": "upgradeMining", "upgrade": "automine" }` is offered only after the tractor beam is owned and costs `mining.automine.cost`. It clears every asteroid, stops further spawns, removes every player miner and the miner queue, and pays `mining.automine.payout` all at once every `mining.automine.everyMs`. Rivals do not fly back to their base. The Miner card hides, and miner purchases and the earlier mining purchases are no longer offered. The Mining button stays. `{ "type": "upgradeMining", "upgrade": "moneyConverter" }` is offered only then and costs `mining.moneyConverter.cost`. Once it is owned, money above `mining.moneyConverter.above` becomes one crystal and the whole balance is spent. The Mining button then hides. `skipLevelOne` does not buy automine or the converter. `{ "type": "unlockDesigner" }` costs `designer.cost` and adds the Ship Designer sidebar. `{ "type": "placeComponent", "component", "x", "y", "rotation" }` bolts that part onto the grid. The click is the part's local (0, 0), rotation is 0, 90, 180, or 270, and a mount must touch the opposite mount of a square already connected to the core. `{ "type": "saveDesign" }` copies that grid into the game. That copy is what the rest of the game uses. Editing the grid does not change it until `saveDesign` runs again. Export writes the grid to `ship-design.json`. Import replaces the grid from that file and leaves the copied production design until `saveDesign`. `{ "type": "resetDesign" }` clears the grid the same way. Delete is a tool: once it is selected, clicking a part takes that part off. `{ "type": "chooseProduction", "design": "fighter" | "saved" }` picks which of those ships the fighter bar builds. `saved` is offered only after a design has been copied. The Fleet Design screen shows that ship's build cost and shape. A saved design flies at its speed times `ships.speed`. Each shot deals `designer.weaponDamage`. Each gun type keeps its own timer. Extra guns of that type shorten its wait. After the designer is owned, the Weapons tab on Ship Upgrades sells a laser and a missile. The laser is long range and spends a whole energy block. Its shot hits immediately and draws a line from the ship to the target, blue for the player and red for a rival. The line stays for 200 ms. Its upgrades raise that range and lower the energy it draws. Those upgrades cost crystals, starting at 5 and doubling. The missile stays hidden until a ship destroys an enemy corvette that carries one. A base cannon kill does not count. `{ "type": "unlockWeapon", "weapon": "missile" }` then costs one crystal. Its fire-rate and damage upgrades cost crystals, starting at 5 and doubling. The missile is medium range. It flies at half the shot speed and is drawn as a longer projectile. One missile starts at 30 damage and fires every 5000 ms. The cannon comes with the designer. It starts at 10 damage and fires every 1000 ms, and the same tab raises its fire rate and damage. The first buy of each cannon upgrade costs money. Later buys cost crystals, starting at 5 and doubling. Ship Upgrades also sells a medium engine and a large engine. They cover more cells, draw more energy, and push harder than the stock thruster. A missile hit also deals half that damage to enemy ships in a round patch of map cells, about three and a half cells from the impact. Those cells light up, then fade. A ship closes until its shortest powered gun is in range, then stops. Longer guns fire on the way in, and only guns that reach add to that type's fire rate. The default fighter still uses the fighter speed, damage, and attack-rate upgrades. It is drawn with the same hull as a miner. Each placed part adds build units, the same production units a fighter takes, and the designer shows that total. Each part also adds weight. Thrusters add thrust. Speed is thrust divided by weight. Every part consumes energy, and energy parts grant more than one part uses. `{ "type": "removeComponent", "id" }` takes a part off when the rest of the ship stays connected to the core. `skipLevelOne` does not buy the designer. `{ "type": "upgradesToShipDesign" }` maxes miner speed, damage, fire rate, carry, count, and production; mining unlock, size, spawn rate, golden asteroids, golden spawn rate, and the tractor beam; fighter speed, attack rate, health, damage, count, and production; and enemy spawn rate, capacity, health, damage, and attack rate. It also unlocks the ship designer, the laser, and the medium and large engines, and it turns on automine. It does not unlock the missile or buy missile upgrades. Each level uses the same effects as buying it, and nothing is charged. An upgrade added later is not on that list. `goldenSpawnRate` is offered only after golden asteroids are owned. `{ "type": "upgradeFighter", "upgrade": "speed" | "attackRate" | "health" | "damage" | "count" | "production" }` buys one of its stats. `count` starts at 0 and is how many fighters are kept. The first purchase is free, the next costs `count.cost`, and the price doubles until `count.max` fighters. After that, `count.crystalLevels` purchases cost crystals, starting at `count.crystal` and doubling, and each adds `count.crystalStep` fighters. A destroyed fighter is built again until that number is full. `production` is units built each second on the fighter bar. A queued fighter costs `fighter.units`. The starting rate fills that bar in 25 seconds. Each production purchase makes the next build take 70% as long. These are offered only after the tractor beam is owned. Speed, attack rate, health, and damage are on Ship Upgrades. Count and production stay on the Default fighter page. That sidebar button hides once both are maxed. The Ship Upgrades button hides once speed, attack rate, health, and damage are maxed. Each fleet bay has its own capacity. Bay 1 uses that count. An extra bay starts with `designer.bayCapacity.start` ships, and further purchases add `step` up to `max`, paid in money that starts at `cost` and doubles. `{ "type": "upgradeEnemy", "upgrade": "spawnRate" | "capacity" | "health" | "damage" | "attackRate" }` is offered only after the tractor beam is owned, when the Enemy tab appears. That screen opens onto Fighter, and Spawn rate and Capacity are on that page. `spawnRate` shortens how long a destroyed rival waits to return. `capacity` shows the live rival fleet cap. That cap starts at 1. Reaching three player miners doubles it, and golden asteroids multiply it by three. The first Capacity purchase sets the cap to 10, and each later purchase adds 5. New ships launch immediately. Health, damage, and attack rate raise the rival ships. Each Capacity, health, damage, and attack-rate purchase multiplies the payout for a destroyed rival by 1.3, and those factors compound. Spawn rate does not. The Enemy menu shows the net multiplier. A ship that destroys a rival pays the fighter bounty times that multiplier, rounded to the nearest dollar. A base cannon kill pays nothing. `{ "type": "skipLevelOne" }` maxes every miner upgrade and every upgrade on the Mining page, including its unlock, without spending money. Levels already bought stay put, and a second use changes nothing. `{ "type": "claimGift" }` pays the sidebar gift. The first payout is `bank.gift`, the next claim pays `bank.giftNext`, and each claim after that doubles. `{ "type": "claimCrystalGift" }` pays the sidebar crystal button. The first payout is `bank.crystalGift`, and each claim doubles the next one.
`act` applies one of those and refuses anything else. The UI and the adapters do not grow their own copies of the rules.

The screen is the Galactica shell: full-height column, 200px sidebar, a
centered main panel, a footer bar, Settings with Dev Mode. Map is the live
view. There is no turn and no end-turn batch. Local play advances from the
animation frame. The HTTP server advances its own game every 100ms.

## State and rendering

`App` owns the `GameClient`, the view, and settings. No store, no context.

The `Game` will be mutated in place. Bump `viewKey` after a change or React
will keep showing the old screen.
